Once Again

A paper-cut story about where the idea of reincarnation came from, how the rumours spread, and why people still believe it. Narrated, with music. Best with sound on.

Sources behind the story
  • Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 4.4.3–5 (c. 700 BCE): the caterpillar simile and "as one acts, so one becomes". Chandogya Upanishad 5.10.
  • Milindapañha (c. 100 BCE): King Milinda and Nagasena, the lamp lit from another lamp.
  • Xenophanes fragment 7, quoted in Diogenes Laërtius 8.36: Pythagoras and the beaten puppy.
  • Plato, Republic book X: the Myth of Er and the plain of Lethe. Phaedo and Meno on recollection.
  • Julius Caesar, Gallic War 6.14: the Druids teach that souls pass from one body to another.
  • Sefer ha-Bahir (12th c.) and Isaac Luria's school (16th c.): gilgul in Jewish mysticism. Druze doctrine of taqammus.
  • Shanti Devi case, Delhi, 1930s; Ian Stevenson, Twenty Cases Suggestive of Reincarnation (1966), University of Virginia.
  • Morey Bernstein, The Search for Bridey Murphy (1956), and the press investigations into Virginia Tighe's childhood that followed. Cryptomnesia research.
  • Pew Research Center surveys: roughly a quarter to a third of U.S. adults say they believe in reincarnation.
