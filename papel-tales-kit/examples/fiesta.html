Mabuhay ang Fiesta

A paper-cut story about where the Filipino fiesta came from: harvest feasts before 1521, the Santo Niño, patron saints, and the open house that feeds everyone. Narrated, with music.

Sources behind the story
  • Antonio Pigafetta, account of Magellan's voyage (1521): baptism of Rajah Humabon and the gift of the Santo Niño in Cebu; the Battle of Mactan.
  • Legazpi expedition records (1565): the Santo Niño found in a burned house in Cebu; Basilica del Santo Niño.
  • Boxer Codex (c. 1590) and William Henry Scott, Barangay: Sixteenth-Century Philippine Culture and Society (1994): pre-colonial feasts, babaylan and offerings to anito and diwata.
  • Reducción policy and the pueblo plan of plaza, church and patron saint under Spanish rule.
  • Sinulog (Cebu), Ati-Atihan (Kalibo, Aklan), Pahiyas (Lucban, Quezon, every 15 May for San Isidro Labrador), MassKara (Bacolod, since 1980).
  • Fernando N. Zialcita and others on the fiesta, bayanihan and the debate over fiesta spending.
