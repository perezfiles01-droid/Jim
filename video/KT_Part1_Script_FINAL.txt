Every day, A D B creates thousands of documents. Which ones must be kept? For how long? And how? That is what E D R M S is for. Welcome to part one of the E D R M S knowledge transfer series.

A D B is the Asian Development Bank, based in Manila, with resident missions and offices around the world.

Some of its documents are important enough to become official records. Records must be kept safe, kept for the right length of time, and disposed of when that time is over.

E D R M S, the Electronic Document and Records Management System, does exactly that. It is built by AvePoint, on SharePoint and Microsoft 365, the tools A D B users already use every day.

So, how does a document become a record? It takes seven simple steps. Let us go through them one by one.

Step one. Open an E D R M S compliant site. This is a SharePoint site that is set up for records. You can see the E D R M S logo at the top.

Step two. Choose a library, and a folder if you need one. The menus at the top list the libraries on this site. Think of a library as a filing cabinet, and a folder as a drawer inside it. In this example, we open the folder called Jim Test 2.

Step three. Create or upload the document. Click Create or upload, and choose your file. A message confirms the upload, and the new file appears in the list.

Step four. Make sure the document has a sensitivity label. A sensitivity label tells everyone how confidential a document is. Regular documents, such as Word or Excel files, need one before they are declared. Audiovisual files, such as images, videos, and email files, do not. Our example is an email file, so it does not need one.

Step five. Physical counterpart. Click the three dots next to the file, and choose Declare as record. E D R M S asks one question. Does this record have a physical counterpart? Choose Yes if a paper copy of it also exists. Choose No if it exists only in electronic form. Here, the answer is No.

Step six. Submit. Click Submit, and wait a moment while E D R M S declares the file. When the message says the file has been successfully declared as a record, you are done. Back in the library, the file now shows a lock icon. A record is protected, so it can no longer be edited or deleted.

Step seven. Check the records. Switch the view to Record Management Review. Here you can check two important columns.

First, the retention label. It tells E D R M S how long the record must be kept. The label depends on the library. Each library is mapped to a retention label, such as five years, seven years, ten years, or permanent. This library keeps records for ten years after declaration.

Second, the sensitivity label. For our email, it is blank, and that is expected, because audiovisual files do not need one. For a Word or Excel file, you would see its sensitivity label here.

And that is it. Seven steps, and the document is now an official A D B record.

The project is delivered as a series of releases. Each release adds one capability, is tested by A D B, and then goes live. This way, A D B gets value step by step, instead of waiting for one big launch.

There are three main groups working on this project. The first is R A C, the records team at A D B. They own the requirements and confirm what each release must do.

The second is I T D, the IT department at A D B. They support the rollout, and their T C S Q A team tests every release before it reaches users.

The third is AvePoint, the delivery team. AvePoint business analysts gather and write the requirements, developers build each release, and AvePoint Q A tests it first.

Now, let us look at the releases. The first release went live in April 2025. Release one point one delivered site and library creation and records declaration, together with the Cloud Governance setup.

In November 2025, Release one point two point one added multimedia records declaration, and disabled the native retention label option, so labels are applied the E D R M S way.

From 2026, releases are numbered by year. Release twenty twenty six point one, in January, added multiple records declaration. Users can now declare many documents at once.

Release twenty twenty six point two, in March, added centralized records declaration. Records can be declared and kept in a central location, not only in the site where they were created.

Release twenty twenty six point three, in June, added email records declaration from Outlook. Its enhancement, release twenty twenty six point three point one, is in progress now.

The current release is twenty twenty six point four, the E D R M S Utilization Report. It shows how departments are using E D R M S, and is planned to go live in December 2026.

After that comes physical records management, now on hold, followed by items on the roadmap such as retention label mapping, records disposition, and site renewal.
