# EDRMS Utilization Report, Release 1: Knowledge Transfer Video Script

Draft 1, 14 October 2026. For narration in ElevenLabs.

**Audience:** colleagues who will use, support or present the report, and have not seen it before.
**Length:** about 9 to 10 minutes of narration (roughly 1,350 words at 145 words a minute).
**Scope:** Release 1 is two dashboards, Bank-wide Oversight and Department Insights. The other four are not in this release and are mentioned only once, in Scene 9.

**How to use this script**
- Only the text under **Narration** goes into ElevenLabs. The **On screen** lines are for whoever records the screen.
- Acronyms are spelled with spaces (E D R M S, A D B, R A C) so the voice reads them letter by letter. Keep that spacing when pasting.
- Each scene is one ElevenLabs generation. Short scenes keep retakes cheap and make it easy to line audio up with the screen recording.
- A blank line inside the narration is a natural pause. For a longer pause, add `<break time="1.0s" />` if your ElevenLabs model supports it.

---

## Scene 1. Why this report exists (about 50 seconds)

**On screen:** Title card, "EDRMS Utilization Report, Release 1". Then the live site landing on Bank-wide Oversight.

**Narration:**

Welcome. In the next ten minutes, you'll learn what the E D R M S Utilization Report is, what is in Release 1, and how to read every part of it with confidence.

First, the why. A D B has moved its records management onto E D R M S, running on SharePoint. Setting up sites is the easy part. The real question is whether people are actually using them. Are documents going in? Are records being declared? And which units are leading, and which need support?

This report answers those questions. It is built for R A C and I T D, and for anyone who needs to track adoption and decide where to follow up.

Release 1 contains two dashboards. Bank-wide Oversight, for the view across the whole Bank. And Department Insights, for one unit at a time. Let's start at the top.

---

## Scene 2. Three words to know first (about 45 seconds)

**On screen:** A simple slide with three terms: Document, Record declared, Physical counterpart.

**Narration:**

Before we look at any numbers, three words you will hear throughout.

A document is any file saved in an E D R M S site.

A record is a document that has been formally declared. Declaring it locks it down as the Bank's official version, under a retention rule.

And a physical counterpart is a declared record that also exists on paper.

One more thing. Throughout the report, a unit means a department, an office, a resident mission, or a regional office. You'll see this written as Department, Office, R M, R O.

---

## Scene 3. Bank-wide Oversight, the four headline cards (about 1 minute 30 seconds)

**On screen:** Bank-wide Oversight. Highlight each of the four cards in turn, then click one to show its breakdown opening below.

**Narration:**

This is Bank-wide Oversight. One question drives this page. How far has E D R M S been adopted across the Bank?

At the top are four headline cards.

Active E D R M S sites. The number of SharePoint sites set up and running as E D R M S sites.

E D R M S users. This shows the approximate size of the workforce in E D R M S units. It is a rounded figure provided by R A C, for context. It is not a live count. The number of people actually active in the system sits inside this card's breakdown, clearly labelled, so the two are never confused.

Documents in E D R M S. Every file held in those sites, with the storage they use.

And records declared. The figure that matters most for recordkeeping.

Here is the most important habit to learn. Every card is clickable. Click one, and its full breakdown opens right underneath. Click it again, and it closes.

In the top corner, you'll also see the data as of date, so you always know how fresh the figures are. And an export button, to take the page into a meeting.

---

## Scene 4. Bank-wide Oversight, sites by unit and physical counterparts (about 1 minute)

**On screen:** Open the Active sites breakdown: top ten chart, then the full table. Scroll to the physical counterparts panel: tiles, donut, table.

**Narration:**

Open the sites card, and you see where those sites are. On the left, a chart of the top ten units. On the right, the full list of every unit. You can search it, sort any column, and export it.

Further down is the physical counterparts panel. These tiles count the declared records that also exist on paper, compare them with all declared records, and show the share that still has a paper version.

The donut gives you that share at a glance. And the table shows it unit by unit, so you can see straight away which offices still rely most on paper.

---

## Scene 5. Bank-wide Oversight, comparison and trend (about 1 minute 15 seconds)

**On screen:** The Comparison panel, switch the selector between documents and records. Then the Records Declaration Trend: try a preset, then a custom range, then switch between month and year.

**Narration:**

Raw totals can mislead. A large department will always hold more documents than a small office. So the comparison panel asks a fairer question.

Choose documents or records declared, and each unit's figure appears beside its approximate headcount. Now you can see whether a unit's use of the system is in proportion to its size.

The last panel on this page is the Records Declaration Trend. Pick a preset, such as the last twelve months or year to date, or set your own date range. You can view it by month or by year.

The bars show how many records were declared in each period. The line is the running total. When that line climbs steadily, adoption is taking hold. When it flattens, that's a conversation worth having.

## Scene 6. Department Insights, the profile and five cards (about 1 minute 15 seconds)

**On screen:** Switch to Department Insights. Open the unit picker and choose a unit. Point to the Go-Live date and site convention. Then highlight the five cards.

**Narration:**

Now let's zoom in. Department Insights tells the same story, one unit at a time.

Start with the picker at the top. Choose a department, office, R M or R O, and everything on the page changes to that unit.

Its profile shows when the unit went live on E D R M S, and the site naming convention it follows.

Below are five cards. Sites. Site visitors, meaning the number of different people who opened the unit's sites in the last 180 days. Documents. Records declared. And physical counterparts.

Just like Bank-wide, every card is clickable, and its breakdown opens right below, site by site.

---

## Scene 7. Department Insights, sites and library usage (about 1 minute)

**On screen:** The sites breakdown: year filter, the table with owner and created date, the yearly chart, click a year to open months. Then scroll to Library usage.

**Narration:**

The sites breakdown lists every site in the unit, with its owner and the date it was created. So if you ever need to know who looks after a site, the answer is here.

You can filter by year or by a custom date range. And the chart shows how many sites were created each year. Click a year, and it opens up into months.

Finally, library usage goes one level deeper, into the document libraries inside each site. For every library, you'll see its documents, records declared, physical counterparts, and active users. This is where you spot which libraries are truly used for recordkeeping, and which are sitting quietly.

---

## Scene 8. Reading the numbers honestly (about 1 minute 15 seconds)

**On screen:** A slide with the four statuses: Agreed and sourced. Agreed, demo figure. Needs clarification. Still lacking. Then point to a "Not captured" cell on screen.

**Narration:**

Now, a very important point. Before you quote any figure from this release, know where it stands.

Every item in Release 1 has one of four statuses.

Agreed and sourced. R A C has agreed it, and the source has been proven. It only needs the production data.

Agreed, demo figure. Agreed, and the source is known. But the number you see today is demonstration data.

Needs clarification. Built, but a decision or definition is still open.

And still lacking. The data source does not exist yet.

Where a figure cannot be produced, the report says Not captured, rather than showing a guess. That is deliberate. A blank you can trust is better than a number you can't.

So please, until production data is loaded, treat the numbers on screen as examples of how the report works, not as the Bank's actual figures.

## Scene 9. What's still open, and what comes next (about 1 minute)

**On screen:** A slide listing the open questions and who owns each one.

**Narration:**

A few questions are still open, and each one has an owner.

From R A C, we need the real headcount for each unit, the Go-Live dates and site conventions, whether staff, consultants and contractors can be split from Active Directory, and confirmation of the 180 day inactivity threshold.

From Cloud Governance, we need the production workspace export. This is the single most valuable file for the report.

From the Microsoft 365 administrators, we need the production SharePoint usage and activity reports.

Once these arrive, the demo figures are replaced with real ones. No redesign is needed.

The other four dashboards, Project Insights, the Institutional File Plan, Retention and Disposal, and Records and Archive Holdings, are planned for later releases, once R A C has made its decisions on them.

---

## Scene 10. Wrap up (about 40 seconds)

**On screen:** Back to Bank-wide Oversight. End card with the link to the report and a contact for questions.

**Narration:**

Let's recap. Bank-wide Oversight shows adoption across the whole Bank. Department Insights shows it one unit at a time. Every card is clickable. Check the data as of date. And always check an item's status before you quote a number.

Thank you for watching. Open the report, choose your own unit, and explore. If anything is unclear, reach out to the project team.
