BATANG 90s: WHY FILIPINOS LOVE THE NINETIES. VOICEOVER SCRIPT (DRAFT FOR APPROVAL)
====================================================================================
Narrator: Kokoro af_heart (American female), speed 0.92
New characters: Tito Jun-jun, a grown-up Philippine long-tailed macaque who was a 90s kid
(sando, band-aid on his cheek, slingshot in his back pocket), and Mika, his niece, a young
Palawan pangolin who rolls into a ball when she laughs.
Length: about 4.5 minutes, 10 scenes, 32 lines. Visual beats: 92 (every 1-2 seconds).
Styles: comic-book thick outlines (from your Batang 90s images), cardboard, crayon,
watercolour, candlelit silhouettes, TV-screen panels, grey cut-outs, collage.

--- PROLOGUE ---  (cardboard living room, night)
Line 1. One rainy evening, Mika the pangolin found her Tito Jun-jun laughing at his phone, wiping tears from his eyes.
   beats: rain streaks on the window / "Mika" rolls in as a ball and uncurls / "Tito Jun-jun" phone glow lights his face / "laughing" he shakes / "tears" two drops fly off
Line 2. "Tito, why do you keep sharing pictures of slippers and marbles? And what is a Batang 90s?"
   beats: phone screen fills the frame: post of a slipper / scroll to marbles / "Batang 90s" stamp drops on the post / Mika tilts head
Line 3. Tito Jun-jun put down his phone and grinned. "Sit down, Mika. Let me take you back to the nineteen nineties."
   beats: phone slides away / grin / "nineteen nineties" a calendar flips fast back from 2026 to 1990 and the room fades to sunny street

--- WHAT IS A BATANG 90s? ---  (crayon on construction paper)
Line 4. Batang 90s means a child of the nineties: Filipinos who grew up in the nineteen nineties, now mostly in their thirties and forties.
   beats: "child" a crayon kid pops up / "nineties" big 9 and 0 cut-outs drop / "grew up" kid grows taller / "thirties and forties" kid becomes an adult with a briefcase
Line 5. Online, they share memes, old photos and inside jokes that only their generation seems to understand.
   beats: three photo cards fan out / "memes" a laughing face sticker / "inside jokes" a speech bubble with "?!" / "their generation" a crowd of crayon heads nods
Line 6. But to understand the obsession, you have to see what a typical day looked like.
   beats: magnifying glass slides across / "typical day" a sun rises over crayon rooftops

--- THE STREET WAS THE PLAYGROUND ---  (comic-book style, thick outlines)
Line 7. After school, children ran outside barefoot or in rubber slippers, and the whole street became a playground.
   beats: school bell rings / kids burst out of a door / "barefoot" bare feet run / "slippers" tsinelas flap / "playground" chalk lines draw themselves on the road
Line 8. They played patintero across lines drawn on the road, and tumbang preso, knocking down a can with a slipper.
   beats: "patintero" kids dodge across lines / "tumbang preso" a can on the ground / "slipper" a thrown slipper flies and the can tumbles
Line 9. Girls and boys hopped through piko, kicked a sipa high into the air, and leapt over hands in luksong tinik.
   beats: "piko" hopscotch boxes and a hop / "sipa" a feathered washer kicked up and spinning / "luksong tinik" a kid leaps over stacked hands
Line 10. When the street lights came on, mothers called them home, and nobody wanted to be the first to leave.
   beats: street lamp flickers on / "mothers" a window opens with a calling mom / kids freeze, look at each other / one waves goodbye slowly

--- POCKET TREASURES ---  (watercolour cut-outs on a wooden table)
Line 11. Their pockets were full of treasures: teks cards with comic pictures, glass marbles called jolens, and a wooden spinning top, the trumpo.
   beats: pocket turns inside out / "teks" a stack of picture cards fans out / "jolens" marbles roll and clink / "trumpo" top drops and spins
Line 12. Some carried a slingshot, the tirador, and many wore a band-aid from the last fall off a bike.
   beats: "tirador" slingshot stretches and snaps / "band-aid" sticks onto a cheek / "bike" a bicycle wheel rolls past and wobbles
Line 13. In 1996, Japan launched the Tamagotchi, a tiny digital pet that beeped for food, and later, kids raced little motorized cars on plastic tracks.
   beats: "1996" date stamp / "Tamagotchi" egg-shaped pet with a pixel creature / "beeped" beep lines / "cars" toy cars zoom around a looped track

--- BROWNOUT NIGHTS ---  (candlelit silhouettes on warm orange)
Line 14. In the early nineteen nineties, the country faced a serious power crisis, with brownouts that could last eight to ten hours a day.
   beats: lights in a row of houses go out one by one / "power crisis" a power pole sparks / "eight to ten hours" a clock spins
Line 15. In 1993, Congress passed the Electric Power Crisis Act to speed up new power plants, and the long brownouts eased.
   beats: "1993" stamp / "Congress" a gavel bangs / "power plants" smokestacks rise / "eased" house windows glow back on
Line 16. For many kids, those dark nights meant candles, hand shadows on the wall, and ghost stories with cousins.
   beats: "candles" flame flickers / "hand shadows" a dog shadow, then a bird shadow on the wall / "ghost stories" a sheet ghost silhouette wobbles, kids hug

--- AFTER-SCHOOL TV ---  (TV-screen panels, geometric)
Line 17. When the power was on, families gathered around one television.
   beats: TV switches on with a dot that grows / family silhouettes lean in
Line 18. Educational shows made learning fun: Sineskwela began in 1994, and Hiraya Manawari followed in 1995.
   beats: "Sineskwela" a test tube bubbles on screen / "1994" stamp / "Hiraya Manawari" a storybook opens with a dragon / "1995" stamp
Line 19. Afternoons also meant cartoons dubbed into Tagalog, and everyone talked about the same episode the next day at school.
   beats: "cartoons" a robot and a hero bounce on screen / "Tagalog" a speech bubble "Ay!" / "next day at school" kids in a classroom whisper in a chain

--- THE TEXTING GENERATION ---  (grey cut-outs, phone screens)
Line 20. By the end of the decade, mobile phones that could send text messages had reached the country.
   beats: chunky phone drops in / signal bars rise / an envelope icon blinks
Line 21. Texting was cheap and quick, and by 2001, Filipinos were sending tens of millions of messages every day.
   beats: thumbs tap fast / "2001" stamp / message envelopes multiply and fly across the frame
Line 22. Newspapers began calling the Philippines the text messaging capital of the world.
   beats: newspaper spins in / headline "TEXT CAPITAL" / a globe with the Philippines glowing

--- WHY WE FEEL NOSTALGIA ---  (gritty grey cut-outs, painted faces)
Line 23. So why do grown-ups miss all this so much? Scientists who study nostalgia have some answers.
   beats: grown-up looks out a window / "scientists" a researcher with a clipboard / "nostalgia" a glowing memory bubble
Line 24. In 2006, psychologists Tim Wildschut, Constantine Sedikides and their colleagues found that people often feel nostalgic when they are lonely or down.
   beats: "2006" stamp / two researcher cut-outs / "lonely" a figure alone on a bench / "down" a small rain cloud
Line 25. Remembering those days made people feel closer to others, better about themselves, and happier.
   beats: the bench figure is joined by friends / "better about themselves" chest puffs up / "happier" the rain cloud turns into sun
Line 26. Memory researchers also found that people remember their teens and early twenties more than any other time of life, so the nineties shine brightly for this generation.
   beats: a timeline of life / "teens and early twenties" that part glows / "nineties" a 90s star lights up on it

--- WHY IT STILL MATTERS ---  (collage board)
Line 27. Batang 90s grew up just before smartphones and social media filled every hour of the day.
   beats: a tsinelas card and a smartphone card face off / a clock fills with app icons
Line 28. So when they share a picture of a slipper or a marble, they are really sharing a feeling: long afternoons, friends outside, and a simpler pace.
   beats: "slipper" card / "marble" card / "feeling" a heart pops / "friends outside" mini street scene / "simpler pace" a slow-swinging hammock
Line 29. "So it is not really about the toys," said Mika. "It is about who you played with."
   beats: toys fade back / kid faces appear in the cards / Mika nods

--- EPILOGUE ---  (comic style street, sunset)
Line 30. "Exactly," said Tito Jun-jun. He found an old can, set it in the middle of the road, and handed Mika a slipper.
   beats: "can" set down with a clink / "slipper" handed over / Mika's eyes sparkle
Line 31. Mika threw, the can went flying, and children from the neighborhood came running to join in.
   beats: slipper flies / can tumbles / "children" kids pour in from both sides / laughter lines
Line 32. And just like that, on one small street, the nineties came back to play.
   beats: camera pulls back / sunset / "nineties" the 9-0 cut-outs drop into the sky like a sign / title card returns
